Key convergence points
----------------------

1. The dormant SSN problem is universal.

Cox, DC14, DC23, and DCSM all independently identify the same vulnerability:
SSNs that aren't being actively used by anyone are the easiest to steal. Cox
targeted homeless people. DC23 describes creating "shelf babies" with fresh
SSNs. DCSM describes using children's SSNs from healthcare breaches. Database
Standard's dormancy detection is the single check that catches all of these.

2. Real documents beat fake documents.

Cox didn't use novelty IDs (DC14's $100 fakes). He got real, state-issued
licenses from actual DMVs. DC23's shelf baby also produces real documents. This
is why Gov ID verification alone isn't enough — the documents ARE authentic.
You need the database corroboration layer behind it.

3. Serial identity creation is a pattern.

Cox created 27 identities. DC23's shelf baby framework creates unlimited
identities. DCSM's serial account opener created 700/day. Graph queries and
face lists are the only thing that catches the 2nd+ attempt. The first one may
pass — but the pattern detection kicks in after that.

What Cox did that NO talk covers
---------------------------------

- Forged mortgage satisfactions — fabricating county recorder documents to
  clear liens. This is entirely outside digital identity verification scope.

- Insider access to victim PII — Cox was a licensed mortgage broker with
  legitimate access to clients' SSNs. DC16 covers insider threats in healthcare
  but not in financial services.

- Social manipulation of DMV staff — Cox charmed his way through in-person DMV
  visits. The DEF CON talks focus on digital/system exploits, not in-person
  social engineering at government offices.

These gaps reinforce why the matthew_cox.md design includes the "out of scope"
note for county records fraud — Persona can't catch everything, but it catches
the identity verification layer comprehensively.
